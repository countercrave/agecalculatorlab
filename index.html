The Science & Law of Age

Frequently Asked Questions

Clarifying leap-year boundaries, psychometric borrowing protocols, pediatric premature milestones, and client-side data security.

How are leap day (February 29) birthdays handled mathematically and legally?
Under traditional English common law and United States precedent, individuals born on February 29 legally attain their new age on the first moment of March 1 in non-leap years. In statutory jurisdictions (like UK birth registration guidelines), completion occurs on February 28. Our primary engine displays 25y 11m 30d on February 28 and completes 26y 0d on March 1.
What is the clinical Pearson WISC subtraction borrowing protocol (YY;MM;DD)?
In psychological and educational evaluations (e.g., WISC-V, Woodcock-Johnson, Pearson Q-Global), chronological age must be derived by subtracting Date of Birth from Test Date. When Test Day is less than Birth Day, exactly 30 days are borrowed from the Test Month (regardless of the previous month's actual length in standard psychometrics). When Test Month is less than Birth Month, 12 months are borrowed from the Test Year. The result is documented in standard semicolon format: YY;MM;DD.
How does the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) calculate corrected age for premature infants?
Corrected age subtracts the number of weeks/days a baby was born before their 40-week full-term due date from their chronological age. Per AAP guidelines, developmental milestones and growth curves use corrected age until the child reaches 24 months (2 years) of chronological age, when developmental catch-up is typically expected.
Why is the canine epigenetic DNA methylation formula more accurate than the 7x rule?
Research conducted at the University of California San Diego (UCSD) proved that dog genomes age non-linearly. The formula Human Equivalent = 16 * ln(Dog Age) + 31 reflects that puppies mature rapidly in year 1 (reaching the equivalent of ~31 human years), with aging deceleration thereafter adjusted by canine breed weight classes.
How does the SSA January 1 birthday rule affect Full Retirement Age?
Under SSA Program Operations Manual System (POMS) guidelines, individuals born on January 1 are legally deemed to have attained their age on December 31 of the previous year. For example, a person born on January 1, 1960 is treated as born in 1959, giving them a Full Retirement Age of 66 years and 10 months rather than age 67.

Pure TypeScript Delta Engine

Zero-dependency calendar-aware chronological calculation:

function computeAge(dob, ref = new Date()) {
  let y = ref.getFullYear() - dob.getFullYear();
  let m = ref.getMonth() - dob.getMonth();
  let d = ref.getDate() - dob.getDate();
  if (d < 0) { m--; d += new Date(ref.getFullYear(), ref.getMonth(), 0).getDate(); }
  if (m < 0) { y--; m += 12; }
  return { years: y, months: m, days: d };
}
