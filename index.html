Quick answer

What is an age calculator?

An age calculator finds the exact calendar time between a date of birth and a selected reference date. The result is shown in completed years, calendar months and remaining days.

Unlike a simple “days ÷ 365” estimate, this method accounts for 28-, 29-, 30- and 31-day months as well as leap years.

FormulaReference date − Date of birth= Years · Months · Days

Why accuracy changes

Why two age calculators can disagree

The difference is usually a date convention, not a decimal rounding error.

01

Real month lengths

February and 30-day months cannot be treated as a flat 31-day block.

02

Leap-day handling

February 29 needs a defined rule when the comparison year is not a leap year.

03

The right reference date

Frequently asked questions

Age Calculator FAQ

Short answers about the calculation method, privacy, leap years and official use.

How does the age calculator work?

It counts complete calendar years, then complete calendar months, then the remaining days between the date of birth and the selected reference date. It uses real month lengths and Gregorian leap-year rules.

What does exact age mean?

Exact age is the elapsed calendar interval between a date of birth and a reference date, expressed as completed years, months and remaining days rather than a rounded decimal.

Can I calculate my age on a past or future date?

Yes. Change the Calculate on field from today to any valid past or future reference date that falls on or after the date of birth.

How are February 29 birthdays handled?

The calculator uses calendar-safe date arithmetic. It preserves February 29 in leap years and uses the last valid day of February when a comparison year does not contain February 29.

Is my date of birth stored?

No. The calculation runs in JavaScript on your device. The site does not need to send the dates to a server to calculate the result.

Can I use the result for an official decision?

Use the result as supporting arithmetic. A school, clinic, employer, court or government agency may apply its own cutoff, rounding or eligibility rules, which take precedence.
