UPSC CSE Age Limit 2027: Category-Wise Limits, Attempts and Birth-Date Windows

The UPSC age rule is three lines long and misunderstood every year. Your age is counted on 1 August of the exam year, the minimum is 21 with no relaxation, and the maximum is the age you must not yet have attained on that date. This guide works through every category, the attempts that run alongside the age window, and the exact birth-date windows for CSE 2026 and 2027.

Quick answer

For UPSC CSE 2027, a General or EWS candidate must have been born between 2 August 1995 and 1 August 2006, and gets 6 attempts. OBC candidates (non-creamy layer) get until 35 years and 9 attempts. SC and ST candidates get until 37 years with no cap on attempts. PwBD candidates get 10 extra years on top of their category limit — 42, 45 or 47 years — with 9 attempts, or unlimited attempts for PwBD SC and ST. Age is counted on 1 August 2027, not on the Prelims date.

The single most useful habit is to check your birth date against the window, not your age against a round number. “I am 32” is ambiguous; “I was born on 1 August 1995” decides CSE 2027 immediately — that candidate is one day too old, while a candidate born on 2 August 1995 is eligible.

The date that decides everything: 1 August

Every UPSC age condition is reckoned on 1 August of the exam year. For CSE 2027 that is 1 August 2027 — after the Prelims in May 2027 and just before the Mains. The notification states the rule as “must have attained 21 years and must not have attained the upper age limit” on that date, and both halves matter.

“Must have attained 21” means you must already be 21 on 1 August. A candidate who turns 21 in September 2027 is not eligible for CSE 2027, even though they will be 21 by the Mains and the interview. Their first eligible year is 2028.

“Must not have attained the upper limit” means you must still be under it. A General candidate who turns 32 on 2 August 2027 is 31 on 1 August 2027 and eligible for CSE 2027. A General candidate who turned 32 on 1 August 2027 itself is not eligible — they have attained 32 on the reckoning date. One day on either side of 1 August decides a whole year, which is why the notification publishes birth-date windows rather than leaving candidates to do the subtraction.

The exam date itself never enters the calculation. A candidate who turns 32 between 1 August 2027 and the Prelims would still be eligible, because the Prelims date is irrelevant — but note the direction: the Prelims for CSE 2027 happen in May 2027, before the reckoning date, so the realistic confusion runs the other way, with candidates who turn 21 or 32 between the Prelims and 1 August.

Category-wise limits and relaxations

General and EWS candidates share the same window: 21 to 32 years, 6 attempts. EWS, introduced through the 103rd Constitutional Amendment in 2019, gives 10% reservation in vacancies to candidates from economically weaker General-category families (annual family income below ₹8 lakh from all sources). It gives no additional age relaxation and no extra attempts — the only benefit is the reservation itself.

OBC candidates in the non-creamy layer get 3 years of relaxation, taking the upper limit to 35, and 9 attempts. The creamy-layer distinction is decisive: an OBC candidate in the creamy layer is treated as General for both age and attempts, so a creamy-layer candidate who plans around 35 has planned around a limit they do not have.

SC and ST candidates get 5 years of relaxation, taking the upper limit to 37, and there is no cap on attempts. “Unlimited” is real but not infinite: only the age limit binds, so an SC candidate who is 37 on 1 August has no attempts left even though the rule book never gave a number.

PwBD relaxation is 10 years and it stacks on top of the category relaxation. That gives 42 years for PwBD General and EWS, 45 for PwBD OBC, and 47 for PwBD SC and ST. Attempts for PwBD candidates are 9 for General, EWS and OBC, and unlimited for SC and ST. Eligible ex-servicemen get up to 5 further years, subject to the service conditions in the notification, with attempts as per their base category.

The old Jammu and Kashmir domicile concession, which appeared in earlier examination cycles, does not appear in the recent notices, including CSE 2026. Candidates who remember it from older guides should check the notification of the year they apply for rather than relying on it.

Attempts: the second clock

Age is only one of the two clocks. An attempt is counted when you appear for the Prelims — sitting even one paper counts. Filling the form, paying the fee, downloading the admit card, or staying home on exam day does not count. A registered-but-not-appeared year has cost you nothing, and a year you sat one paper in has cost you one attempt whether you finished the paper or not.

The attempt cap often binds before the age limit does. A General candidate who starts at 21 and attempts every year exhausts 6 attempts by age 26, with years of age eligibility still remaining but no attempts left. An OBC candidate starting at 21 exhausts 9 attempts by age 29, six years before the age window closes. Planning around the age window alone therefore overstates how many chances you actually have.

For SC and ST candidates the planning problem is the reverse: attempts are unlimited, so the whole question is the age window, and the last eligible year deserves the most preparation, not the least. For everyone else, the binding constraint is whichever runs out first — attempts or age — and the calculator on this site shows both so the tighter one is visible.

Birth-date windows for CSE 2026 and 2027

For CSE 2027, the windows are as follows. General and EWS: born between 2 August 1995 and 1 August 2006. OBC: born between 2 August 1992 and 1 August 2006. SC and ST: born between 2 August 1990 and 1 August 2006. PwBD General and EWS: born between 2 August 1985 and 1 August 2006. PwBD OBC: born between 2 August 1982 and 1 August 2006. PwBD SC and ST: born between 2 August 1980 and 1 August 2006. In every case the latest birth date is the same — 1 August 2006, the date that makes you exactly 21 on 1 August 2027 — and only the earliest date moves with the relaxation.

For CSE 2026 the General window, published in the notification, is 2 August 1994 to 1 August 2005. The other categories shift by exactly one year from their 2027 windows: OBC from 2 August 1991, SC and ST from 2 August 1989, PwBD General and EWS from 2 August 1984, PwBD OBC from 2 August 1981, and PwBD SC and ST from 2 August 1979, all to 1 August 2005. The pattern is the rule itself: earliest date 2 August of (exam year minus upper limit), latest date 1 August of (exam year minus 21).

Two boundary cases show why the exact day matters. A General candidate born on 2 August 1995 is eligible for CSE 2027 and it is also their last eligible year — they turn 32 on 2 August 2027, one day after the reckoning date. A General candidate born on 1 August 1995 is not eligible for CSE 2027 at all; their last eligible year was 2026. If your birth date is within a few days of either edge, check it against the window rather than trusting a rounded age.

Certificates that decide your category

UPSC takes your date of birth only from your Class 10 (matriculation) or equivalent certificate. Once entered in an application, it is not changed later. If the date on your other documents differs from your Class 10 certificate, the certificate governs — resolve any mismatch long before the application window, not during it.

Category claims need their own certificates: a non-creamy-layer certificate for OBC, a caste certificate for SC and ST, and a benchmark disability certificate for PwBD. The arithmetic on this site checks whether your dates fit a category; the certificates decide whether the category is one you can claim. Both must hold, and a failure at document verification ends a candidacy after the exam, which is the most expensive place to fail.

Ex-serviceman relaxation likewise depends on the service conditions in the notification — length and character of service, and the specific categories of personnel covered. If you are claiming it, read the notification’s conditions rather than a summary, because summaries routinely drop the qualifying conditions.

Planning your attempts across your window

Start from your last eligible year and work backwards. A General candidate born in 2003 is eligible from 2024 to 2034 — eleven cycles — but has only 6 attempts. Burning one every year from 2024 exhausts them by 2029, five years before the window closes. The better plan is usually to attempt only when preparation justifies it, keeping attempts in reserve for the years when you are strongest.

An OBC candidate born in 1998 is eligible from 2019 to 2033 with 9 attempts — a long window, but the same logic applies with a slightly larger budget. An SC candidate born in 1990 is eligible from 2011 to 2027 with unlimited attempts, so the constraint is purely the window: 2027 is the last year, and preparation should peak there rather than being spread evenly.

The practical rule: never spend an attempt on a year you have not prepared for, because an attempt spent is an attempt gone, while an unspent year costs nothing. And never assume the window will extend — age limits have been stable, but they are set by each year’s notification, and the notification of the year you apply for is the only authoritative document.

What changes each year — and what to re-check

The structure — 21 minimum, 32 General maximum, 1 August reckoning — has been stable across recent cycles, but the dates move every year and the notification is reissued every year. As per the published UPSC annual calendar, the CSE 2027 notification is scheduled for January 2027, with the Prelims on 23 May 2027 and the Mains from August 2027. When the notification releases, re-check three things: the reckoning date, the relaxation table, and whether any concession has been added or dropped.

Two things changed recently enough to matter. The Jammu and Kashmir domicile concession disappeared from recent notices, and PwBD stacking is now stated explicitly as cumulative with the category relaxation. Both are the kind of detail that old guides get wrong, which is why this guide dates its rules to the CSE 2026 notification and the published 2027 calendar rather than presenting them as timeless.

If you are reading this after the CSE 2027 notification has released, treat that document as authoritative over this guide. The calculator on this site applies the same rules for any exam year you select, but a notification change after publication would not be reflected until this page is updated.

Mistakes that cost a year or an attempt

Counting age on the Prelims date or the application date instead of 1 August. The reckoning date is fixed and it falls after the Prelims, so May-age arithmetic gives the wrong answer in both directions.

Reading “32 years” as “32 completed years allowed”. As the birth windows show, being 32 on 1 August is already too old for General. If your calculation says you are “just 32”, check the exact date — one day decides it.

Claiming OBC relaxation while in the creamy layer, or PwBD relaxation without a benchmark disability certificate. Both fail at document verification, after the exam has been sat.

Counting a registered-but-not-appeared year as an attempt, and so believing attempts are exhausted when they are not — or the reverse, forgetting a year you sat one paper in and planning around attempts you no longer have.

Trusting an old guide’s Jammu and Kashmir concession, or an old notification’s dates. The notification of the year you apply for is the only authoritative document, and this guide’s rules follow the CSE 2026 notification and the published 2027 calendar.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum age for UPSC CSE?

21 years on 1 August of the exam year, for every category. There is no relaxation on the minimum age.

What is the maximum age for a General candidate?

32 years, in the notification’s sense: you must not have attained 32 years on 1 August of the exam year. For CSE 2027 that means being born between 2 August 1995 and 1 August 2006.

Is my age counted on the exam date or on 1 August?

On 1 August of the exam year. The Prelims date in May and the application date do not matter for the age calculation.

What is the age limit for OBC candidates?

35 years, a 3-year relaxation over General, with 9 attempts. It applies to OBC candidates in the non-creamy layer; creamy-layer candidates are treated as General.

What is the age limit for SC and ST candidates?

37 years, a 5-year relaxation, with no cap on the number of attempts. Only the age limit binds.

What is the age limit for PwBD candidates?

The 10-year PwBD relaxation stacks on the category limit: 42 years for PwBD General and EWS, 45 for PwBD OBC, and 47 for PwBD SC and ST. Attempts are 9 for General, EWS and OBC, and unlimited for SC and ST.

How is an attempt counted?

An attempt is counted when you appear for the Prelims — sitting even one paper counts. Filling the form, paying the fee, downloading the admit card, or staying home on exam day does not count.

Do ex-servicemen get an age relaxation?

Eligible ex-servicemen get up to 5 years of relaxation, subject to the service conditions in the notification. Attempts remain as per the base category.

Does the old Jammu and Kashmir domicile relaxation still exist?

It appeared in earlier examination cycles but does not appear in the recent notices, including CSE 2026. Check the notification of the year you apply for rather than relying on it.

What if my date of birth is 29 February?

This site’s calculator counts your birthday as 28 February in non-leap years. UPSC itself uses the date on your Class 10 certificate, so the certificate date is what matters.
