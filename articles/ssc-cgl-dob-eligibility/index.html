SSC CGL DOB Eligibility 2026: Check Your Eligible Date of Birth

SSC CGL date of birth eligibility is determined strictly as on 01 August 2026. Because different post cadres have distinct age brackets (18–27, 20–30, 18–30, and 18–32 years), there is no single universal cutoff date—your eligible birth date window depends directly on the cadre you target.

Crucially, the Staff Selection Commission verifies candidate age exclusively against the Date of Birth recorded in your 10th / Matriculation Certificate (Aadhaar cards or affidavits are not accepted). Check your post-wise permissible DOB corridor and verify your eligibility below.

Official Rule Summary

What Is the Eligible DOB for SSC CGL 2026?

There is no single universal date of birth cutoff for every SSC CGL post because different posts have different statutory age limits. All birth dates are evaluated against the crucial reckoning date of 01 August 2026. For General (Unreserved) candidates, the official DOB corridors are:

  • 18–27 Years (Auditor / Tax Assistant): Born not earlier than 02-08-1999 and not later than 01-08-2008.
  • 20–30 Years (ASO in CSS, MEA, IB, Railway): Born not earlier than 02-08-1996 and not later than 01-08-2006.
  • 18–30 Years (Inspectors in Central Excise, IT, PO): Born not earlier than 02-08-1996 and not later than 01-08-2008.
  • 18–32 Years (Junior Statistical Officer): Born not earlier than 02-08-1994 and not later than 01-08-2008.

How Is SSC CGL DOB Eligibility Determined?

When aspirants ask "Is my date of birth eligible for SSC CGL?", they often make the mistake of calculating their age as of today. In Indian civil service examinations conducted under the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) mandates, age eligibility is a statutory mathematical conversion between two immovable calendar coordinates: your official date of birth and the gazetted age-reckoning date.

To understand whether your date of birth permits you to submit an application without risking rejection during scrutiny or Document Verification (DV), the Staff Selection Commission follows a rigid four-stage evaluation sequence:

  1. Step 1 — Identify Targeted Post Cadre: Determine the statutory age bracket (e.g. 18–27, 20–30, 18–30, or 18–32) for the specific vacancy code you intend to prioritize. Refer to our foundational guide on SSC CGL age limits for complete department-wise classifications.
  2. Step 2 — Lock the Crucial Reckoning Date: For the SSC CGL 2026 recruitment cycle, DoPT Office Memorandums specify 01 August 2026 as the benchmark date.
  3. Step 3 — Calculate the Statutory DOB Boundary Corridor: Subtract the maximum and minimum completed years from 01 August 2026 to generate the "Not Earlier Than" and "Not Later Than" boundaries.
  4. Step 4 — Match Your Matriculation Certificate DOB: Your birth date as registered on your Class 10 board certificate must fall squarely on or between these two boundary dates, subject to any statutory category relaxations.

Why Is 01 August 2026 Important for SSC CGL?

One of the most persistent misunderstandings among government exam aspirants is assuming that age is calculated on the day an advertisement is released or on the day they click "Submit" on the SSC portal. This assumption leads thousands of candidates to miscalculate their eligibility every year.

01 AUG 2026

The Universal Crucial Reckoning Date

Under DoPT guidelines, examinations conducted in the second half of a calendar year benchmark candidate eligibility against 01 August. Every birth date calculation for SSC CGL 2026 is anchored to this single calendar day.

Consider a candidate born on 15 September 1995 applying for an Inspector post (maximum age 30 years). If this candidate calculates their age in June 2026 while filling out the form, they might celebrate being 30 years and 9 months old, assuming they still have time. However, on 01 August 2026, they are 30 years, 10 months, and 17 days old. Because they exceeded 30 completed years on the cutoff date, they are overage under the Unreserved quota.

Which Date of Birth Is Valid for SSC CGL?

A common crisis during the Document Verification (DV) phase involves candidates whose birth dates differ between their government ID documents. Some candidates enter the date from their Aadhaar card or PAN card, only to find their application summarily canceled during final scrutiny.

The Staff Selection Commission’s position is unambiguous: The Date of Birth entered in the online application form must match identically with the date recorded in the Matriculation or Secondary Examination Certificate.

📜

1. Matriculation Record

Your 10th Class Board Certificate is the single legally recognized primary source of truth for age verification.

📝

2. Online Application Form

The DOB typed into the SSC registration form must be an exact character-by-character copy of the 10th record.

⚖️

3. Statutory Scrutiny

Any discrepancy between your form DOB and certificate DOB results in immediate disqualification; affidavits are not accepted.

🏆

4. Final DV Clearance

Original 10th marksheet and passing certificate are physically audited by the user department before appointment.

Crucial Warning Regarding Aadhaar & Passport:

If your Aadhaar card or passport displays a date of birth that differs from your 10th Board Certificate, do NOT enter the Aadhaar date. SSC will reject any request for post-submission amendment. Always enter the exact date shown on your 10th certificate.

What If My Date of Birth Is Exactly on the SSC CGL Cutoff Date?

Boundary date cases generate intense anxiety among candidates. Hundreds of queries arise every cycle from applicants whose birthdays fall on the exact 1st or 2nd of August.

The legal interpretation of SSC cutoff phrasing is governed by established Supreme Court jurisprudence regarding civil service age reckoning:

  • "Born not earlier than 02-08-1996": A candidate born on 02 August 1996 is eligible. However, a candidate born on 01 August 1996 is NOT eligible under the Unreserved category because they completed 30 years on 31 July 2026 and were already into their 31st year on the crucial cutoff day.
  • "Born not later than 01-08-2008": A candidate born on 01 August 2008 attains 18 completed years on 01 August 2026 and is statutorily eligible. Conversely, a candidate born on 02 August 2008 turns 18 on 02 August 2026, meaning they are underage by exactly 24 hours on the crucial date.

Why Your Current Age May Not Be Enough

When an applicant uses a generic online age calculator, it outputs their age based on current system time (today's date). However, using today's age to assess SSC CGL eligibility is fundamentally flawed.

Consider a candidate evaluating their eligibility on 10 March 2026 whose Date of Birth is 15 May 1996:

  • Age Today (10 March 2026): 29 Years, 9 Months, 23 Days → Appears eligible for a 30-year post!
  • Age on Crucial Date (01 August 2026): 30 Years, 2 Months, 17 Days → Statutorily OVERAGE for Unreserved quota!

This 5-month disparity is why generic calculators produce devastating false positives. Always evaluate your date of birth using a purpose-built tool like our SSC CGL Age Calculator that anchors calculations to 01 August 2026.

Can Age Relaxation Change My Eligible DOB?

Yes. Statutory reservations provide upper-age relaxations that systematically extend the earliest permissible Date of Birth further back into earlier years. Importantly, age relaxation only alters the earliest permitted DOB; the latest permitted DOB (minimum entry age of 18 or 20) never changes.

For the standard 18–30 Inspector bracket, observe how category relaxations shift the permissible DOB corridor:

  • General / UR (Base Band): 02 August 1996 – 01 August 2008 (Max 30 years)
  • OBC (+3 Years Relaxation): 02 August 1993 – 01 August 2008 (Max 33 years)
  • SC / ST (+5 Years Relaxation): 02 August 1991 – 01 August 2008 (Max 35 years)
  • PwBD (Unreserved, +10 Years): 02 August 1986 – 01 August 2008 (Max 40 years)
  • PwBD (OBC, +13 Years): 02 August 1983 – 01 August 2008 (Max 43 years)
  • PwBD (SC/ST, +15 Years): 02 August 1981 – 01 August 2008 (Max 45 years)

Common SSC CGL DOB Mistakes to Avoid

Every recruitment cycle, minor oversights in birth date calculation lead to rejected applications or post-selection cancellation. Review these seven frequent pitfalls:

MISTAKE 01

Using Today's Date

Calculating your current age instead of computing completed years on the gazetted crucial date of 01 August 2026.

MISTAKE 02

Using Aadhaar Card DOB

Typing an Aadhaar DOB that conflicts with your 10th Board Certificate. SSC rejects all non-matriculation dates.

MISTAKE 03

Assuming Universal 32 Max

Believing all posts permit candidates up to 32 years. 32 is strictly reserved for JSO; most posts cap at 27 or 30.

MISTAKE 04

Ignoring Post Cadres

Selecting 18–27 clerical posts when you are already 28, causing automatic system rejection at the scrutiny stage.

MISTAKE 05

Misinterpreting 24-Hour Boundary

Assuming 02 August 2008 qualifies because it is "August 2008". Missing the cutoff by 1 day invalidates eligibility.

MISTAKE 06

Overlooking Relaxation Window

Applying under UR quota when your category relaxation (OBC, SC, ST) was necessary to validate your birth date.

MISTAKE 07

Typo During One-Time Reg

Swapping day and month (e.g. typing 06/07 instead of 07/06). SSC does not grant post-submission amendments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the DOB limit for SSC CGL 2026?
The DOB limit is post-specific. For the 18–27 age band (Auditor, Tax Assistant), the permitted window is 02 August 1999 to 01 August 2008. For 20–30 posts (ASO), it is 02 August 1996 to 01 August 2006. For 18–30 posts (Inspectors), it is 02 August 1996 to 01 August 2008. For 18–32 posts (JSO), it is 02 August 1994 to 01 August 2008.
What is the latest DOB for SSC CGL 2026?
For posts with a minimum entry age of 18 years, the latest permissible Date of Birth is 01 August 2008. For posts requiring a minimum age of 20 years (such as Assistant Section Officer in CSS, MEA, and IB), the latest permissible Date of Birth is 01 August 2006.
What is the earliest DOB for SSC CGL 2026?
For General (Unreserved) candidates, the earliest permissible Date of Birth is 02 August 1994 for the Junior Statistical Officer (JSO) post, 02 August 1996 for Inspector and ASO cadres (30-year max), and 02 August 1999 for 27-year cadres. Category relaxations shift this date earlier by 3 years for OBC and 5 years for SC/ST.
Is 1 August 2008 eligible for SSC CGL?
Yes. Candidates born on 01 August 2008 complete exactly 18 years on the crucial reckoning date of 01 August 2026. They are eligible for all SSC CGL posts requiring a minimum age of 18 years (including Tax Assistant, Auditor, Central Excise Inspector, and JSO).
Is 2 August 2008 eligible for SSC CGL?
No. A candidate born on 02 August 2008 is exactly 17 years and 364 days old on 01 August 2026. They have not completed 18 years on the crucial cutoff date and are ineligible by exactly one day.
Which DOB is accepted by SSC?
SSC accepts exclusively the Date of Birth recorded in the Matriculation or Secondary Examination Certificate (10th Board Certificate). Conflicting dates on Aadhaar cards, PAN cards, municipal birth certificates, or affidavits are not accepted.
Does SSC CGL DOB change based on category?
Yes. Statutory upper-age relaxations extend the earliest permissible Date of Birth for reserved categories. For example, in the 18–30 band, OBC candidates (+3 years) can be born as early as 02 August 1993, and SC/ST candidates (+5 years) can be born as early as 02 August 1991. The latest permissible DOB remains unchanged.
Can I change my DOB after submitting the SSC CGL form?
No. The official notification explicitly states that once submitted, no request for modification of Date of Birth will be entertained or granted under any circumstances.
Does SSC CGL use today's age?
No. SSC CGL never evaluates age based on current calendar time or form submission date. All age requirements are evaluated strictly against the notification's crucial reckoning date of 01 August 2026.
How can I calculate my SSC CGL eligible DOB?
