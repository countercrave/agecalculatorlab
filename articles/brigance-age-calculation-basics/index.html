BRIGANCE Age Calculation: What the Calculator Does and Doesn't Do

BRIGANCE screening workflows need chronological age on the screening date, in the format the product materials specify.

Quick answer

Use the calculator for the calendar arithmetic and the current BRIGANCE manual for the age rule, the cutoffs and every interpretation that follows.

What the arithmetic has to get right

The calculation is exact calendar age from the child's documented date of birth to the screening date: complete years, then complete months, then the remaining days. There is no BRIGANCE-specific arithmetic — the difficulty is in doing ordinary calendar subtraction reliably, under time pressure, from a record that may be handwritten.

The failure modes are mundane and consistent: a transposed day and month, a borrow step done with a flat thirty days, a birth year misread from a photocopy. None of these is caught by a correct formula, which is why input verification against the source record matters more than the method.

Use this guide with the right tool: Open the BRIGANCE Age Calculator for chronological age preparation for BRIGANCE assessment records. If the question shifts, compare it with the Chronological Age Calculator or the Assessment Age Calculator.

Screening cutoffs are where days matter

Developmental screening tools organise items and criteria by narrow age ranges, particularly in the first few years where change is rapid. A child close to a boundary can fall either side of it on a one-day difference, and the resulting item set is different.

That is an argument for copying dates directly from the record rather than typing them from memory or from a second-hand note. Where the date of birth is estimated rather than documented — which happens — the estimate should be flagged in the record, because every downstream interpretation inherits it.

The calculator is not the manual

This is the line worth being explicit about. A general age calculator produces a calendar interval. It does not know which BRIGANCE product you are using, which edition, what its age ranges are, how it wants the age formatted, when a correction for prematurity applies, or what any result means.

All of that lives in the current product materials. Using a calculator output as though it carried BRIGANCE authority is a category error, and it is the specific risk that comes with tools named after an instrument. The name describes the workflow the tool supports, not an endorsement or an implementation of the instrument.

Prematurity and documentation

Where a child was born preterm, whether and how to adjust age for screening is a decision set by the product materials and by the professional running the screen. It varies by instrument and by the child's current age. Calculate chronological age first, then apply whatever correction the materials direct — and keep both figures.

Record the date of birth, the screening date, the resulting age, whether a correction was applied, and the source of the rule. Anyone reviewing the file later, including in a dispute, should be able to rebuild the number without contacting the person who ran the screen.

Worked example

A child born 8 January 2023 screened on 16 August 2026 is 3 years, 7 months and 8 days old. Screened a week earlier, on 9 August, the same child is 3;07;01. Where an instrument's item set changes at three years eight months, neither date reaches it — but a screen on 8 September would.

Work through it with your own dates and the difference will usually be obvious. If it is not, the convention in use is the first thing to check.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a general guide as scoring guidance. This page explains calendar arithmetic. Item selection, scoring and interpretation come from the current BRIGANCE materials.
  • Typing the date of birth from memory or a second-hand note. Copy it from the source record. A correct formula cannot repair a wrong input.
  • Applying a prematurity correction by default. Whether to correct, and for how long, is set by the product materials and professional judgement, not by the calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official BRIGANCE tool?

No. It is an independent calendar-age calculator that supports a BRIGANCE workflow. The current official materials are the authority on every BRIGANCE-specific question.

Does it apply a correction for prematurity?

No. Calculate chronological age here, then apply any correction the materials direct, keeping both figures in the record.

What should I record alongside the age?

Date of birth, screening date, the age, whether a correction was applied, and where the rule came from.
