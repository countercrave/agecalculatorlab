Kindergarten Cutoff Dates: Reading the Rule Precisely

A cutoff is a policy boundary, and the wording around it decides outcomes for children born within a day of it.

Updated August 16, 2026·About 3 minutes·692 words·Original educational guide
Written and maintained byNavjeet KambojAge Calculator Lab author & editor

Quick answer

Find the operative wording, not a summary. 'On or before' and 'before' differ by one day, and that day contains real children.

Where cutoffs come from

In the US, kindergarten entry ages are generally set at state level, with some states delegating to districts. The dates range across the autumn, and several states have moved theirs in recent years, usually earlier.

That means an article from a few years ago, or a parenting forum, is not a reliable source. The state education department or the district's own enrolment page is.

Inclusive or exclusive wording

'A child must turn five on or before 1 September' includes children born exactly on 1 September. 'A child must turn five before 1 September' excludes them. Both formulations are in use and they differ by exactly one day.

Summaries and third-party guides frequently paraphrase away this distinction. If a birth date is close to the cutoff, the operative text of the policy is the only thing worth reading.

Exceptions and early entry

Many districts have a process for early entry, usually involving assessment, and many have a deferral process for children who would be among the youngest. These sit outside the cutoff calculation and have their own deadlines, which are often months before enrolment.

Because the deadlines come early, this is worth investigating in the year before enrolment rather than at enrolment. A process discovered too late is functionally the same as no process.

What a calculator can and cannot settle

It can tell you exactly how old a child will be on a given date, to the day, which removes any arithmetic doubt. That is genuinely useful when comparing a birth date against a cutoff.

It cannot tell you the cutoff, whether the wording is inclusive, whether exceptions apply, or whether a district has changed its policy. Those come from the district, and no general tool has access to them.

Worked example

With a cutoff of 'turns five on or before 1 September', a child born 1 September 2021 turns five on 1 September 2026 and qualifies. Under 'before 1 September', the same child does not. The exact age is identical; only the wording differs.

Try it with the dates in front of you. Where the answer sits close to a threshold, treat that as a prompt to check the rule rather than to conclude.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Reading a summary instead of the policy. Paraphrases routinely lose the inclusive-exclusive distinction, which is the whole question at the boundary.
  • Using last year's cutoff. Several states have changed theirs recently. Check the current publication.
  • Missing an exception deadline. Early-entry and deferral processes often close months before enrolment.

Frequently asked questions

Are cutoffs the same across a state?

Often, but several states delegate to districts. Check the district as well as the state.

What if my child is a few days off?

Verify the birth date against the certificate, verify the cutoff against the current policy, and check the exact wording. Then ask the district about any exception process.

Does the calculator approve enrolment?

No. It computes exact age on a date you choose. Enrolment decisions belong to the district.
