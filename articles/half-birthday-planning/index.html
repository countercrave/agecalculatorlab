Half Birthdays: Six Months, Not 182 Days

A half birthday is six calendar months after your birthday, which is somewhere between 181 and 184 days depending on where it falls.

Quick answer

Add six calendar months, not half a year in days. End-of-month birth dates need a stated clamping rule.

Why it exists

Half birthdays are mostly a solution to a scheduling problem. Children born during school holidays miss the classroom celebration everyone else gets, and marking the half birthday during term restores it. The tradition spread from there into general use.

It is also a convenient excuse for adults with December birthdays, whose actual celebration competes with everything else that month.

Months, not days

Six calendar months from 15 January is 15 July: 181 days. Six months from 15 July is 15 January: 184 days. Neither is half of 365, and adding 182 days lands a day or two off the calendar date in most cases.

The calendar-month method also gives a memorable result — the same day number, six months round — which a day-count method does not.

End-of-month dates

A birthday on the 31st has no matching date six months later in five of the twelve possible destination months. A 31 August birthday would need 31 February, which does not exist; a 31 March birthday needs 31 September, which does not either.

Clamping to the last day of the destination month is the usual answer and what these calculators do. As with any clamping, the thing that causes confusion is switching conventions between years, not the choice itself.

Where it does not apply

Half birthdays have no standing in school cutoffs, eligibility rules, insurance bands or anything else administrative. A child is not 'six and a half' for any rule that asks their age — they are six.

The one place fractional ages do appear formally is developmental and growth charting in early childhood, where age is often expressed in months. That is a different thing: a precise month count, not a half-birthday anniversary.

Worked example

A birthday of 31 August gives a half birthday on 28 or 29 February, because February has no 31st. A birthday of 15 January gives 15 July — 181 days later, while 15 July gives 15 January, 184 days later. Adding 182 days would miss both.

Work through it with your own dates and the difference will usually be obvious. If it is not, the convention in use is the first thing to check.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Adding 182 or 183 days. Six calendar months varies from 181 to 184 days depending on which months it spans.
  • Switching the clamping rule between years. Pick clamping or rolling and keep it, or past dates cannot be reproduced.
  • Using a half birthday for an age question. No rule anywhere recognises half ages. Give the completed age.

Frequently asked questions

What about a 29 February birthday?

Six months gives 29 August, which exists every year — so leap-day birthdays are the one case with no clamping problem here.

Is there a quarter birthday too?

Yes, three calendar months, with the same end-of-month consideration. It is a less established tradition.

Does the weekday matter?

Only for planning. The calculator reports it so you can see whether the date falls somewhere convenient.
