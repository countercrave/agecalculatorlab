What you get

Lifetime screen time at your daily average

Enter a date of birth, the date to calculate on, and an average number of screen hours per day. The calculator multiplies the days lived by that daily average and converts the total hours into estimated days spent on screens.

★Method

Days lived times hours per day

The estimate is a straight line: the total days between the birth date and the calculation date, multiplied by the daily screen hours, divided by 24 to express the result in days. The result is rounded to the nearest whole day.

◎Example

Worked example: born 1 January 2000, at 4 hours a day

From 1 January 2000 to 20 October 2026 is 9,789 days lived. At 4 hours a day that is 9,789 × 4 ÷ 24 — about 1,632 days on screens, or roughly four and a half years of accumulated screen time.

⌁Limits

One average applied to a whole life

The same daily average is applied to every day since birth, including childhood years when screen time was likely lower, so the figure is a what-if estimate rather than a record. Changing the daily average scales the result in proportion — entering 2 hours a day halves it.

Common questions

Screen Time Lived Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

How is lifetime screen time estimated?

In a straight line: the total days between the birth date and the calculation date, multiplied by the average screen hours per day, divided by 24 to express the total in days, and rounded to the nearest whole day.

What does the worked example give?

Someone born on 1 January 2000, calculated on 20 October 2026, has lived 9,789 days. At 4 hours a day that is about 1,632 days on screens — roughly four and a half years of accumulated screen time.

How does changing the daily average change the result?

In direct proportion, because the same average is applied to every day lived. Halving the average halves the estimate: the 1 January 2000 example at 2 hours a day comes to about 816 days instead of about 1,632.

Does it account for childhood, when screen time was likely lower?

No. One average is applied to every day since birth, including the early years when screen time was likely lower, so the figure is a what-if estimate at a steady daily habit rather than a record of screen time actually accumulated.

What does the Calculate on date do?

It sets the end of the span being measured. The days lived are counted from the birth date to that date, so calculating on a later date adds days to the span — and therefore more estimated screen time to the total.
