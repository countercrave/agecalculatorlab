How it works

Get your Heart Age result in four clear steps

Enter your age, sleep, activity level, resting heart rate and smoking status, then select Calculate. The explanation is broken into short actions so you can understand the workflow without reading a wall of text.

1

Chronological age

Enter your real age in years. This is the base figure the habit bands adjust up or down.

2

Average sleep hours

Add your Average sleep hours, Active days per week, Resting heart rate (optional) and Smoking status (No, Former or Current). Leave the heart-rate field blank and a neutral 70 beats per minute is used.

3

Bands weighted to heart-related inputs

Smoking adds 4 years and former smoking 1; a resting heart rate over 90 adds 2 and under 65 subtracts 1; one or no active days adds 2, five or more subtracts 2; sleep under 6 or over 9 hours adds 2, and 7 to 9 hours subtracts 1.

4

Read the estimate

Age 55, sleeping 5 hours, with no active days, a heart rate of 92 and current smoking adds 2 + 2 + 2 + 4 = +10 and reads 65, and the result never falls below 13. It is an educational index only: no blood pressure, cholesterol or medical history is used, so it is not a clinical heart age or a risk score.

What you get

How the heart age estimate is worked out

Enter your chronological age, average sleep hours, active days per week, resting heart rate (optional) and smoking status. The calculator converts those habits into a plus-or-minus adjustment and reports an educational heart age index, with the adjustment shown.

♡Method

Habit bands, weighted to heart-related inputs

The adjustment is a sum of fixed bands: smoking adds 4 years and former smoking 1; a resting heart rate over 90 adds 2 and under 65 subtracts 1; one or no active days adds 2, five or more subtracts 2; sleep under 6 or over 9 hours adds 2, and 7 to 9 hours subtracts 1.

◎Example

Worked example: age 55, sleeping 5 hours, no active days

With a resting heart rate of 92 and current smoking, the bands add 2 for sleep, 2 for inactivity, 2 for heart rate and 4 for smoking, an adjustment of +10, so the heart age estimate is 65 years, ten above the chronological age entered.

⌁Limits

An educational index, not a clinical heart age

Clinical heart-age tools use blood pressure, cholesterol and medical history; this page uses none of them, so the figure is an educational prompt only, not a risk score or a diagnosis. Leaving the heart-rate field blank applies a neutral 70 beats per minute, and the estimate never falls below 13.

Common questions

Heart Age Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

How is the heart age estimate worked out?

It converts your answers into a plus-or-minus adjustment on your chronological age, using bands weighted to heart-related inputs: smoking adds 4 years and former smoking 1; a resting heart rate over 90 adds 2 and under 65 subtracts 1; one or no active days adds 2, five or more subtracts 2; sleep under 6 or over 9 hours adds 2, and 7 to 9 hours subtracts 1. The adjustment is shown with the result.

What heart age does a 55-year-old with unfavourable habits get?

Sleeping 5 hours adds 2, no active days adds 2, a resting heart rate of 92 adds 2 and current smoking adds 4. The adjustment is +10, so the heart age estimate is 65 years, ten above the chronological age entered.

How does this differ from a clinical heart age?

Clinical heart-age tools use blood pressure, cholesterol and medical history; this page uses none of them. The page is explicit that the figure is an educational prompt only, not a risk score or a diagnosis.

What happens if I leave the resting heart rate blank?

A neutral 70 beats per minute is applied, which scores 0 in the heart-rate band. The estimate is also floored at 13, so it never falls below that whatever answers are entered.

Why does exactly 6 hours of sleep score 0?

The sleep bands are under 6 or over 9 hours (+2) and 7 to 9 hours (-1), so exactly 6 hours sits between the bands and scores 0. The same in-between behaviour applies to two to four active days.
