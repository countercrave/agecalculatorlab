Worked example

Worked example

Employment from 12 January 2015 to 30 June 2026. Enter those two dates above and the calculator returns the same duration.

  1. Calendar duration: 11 years, 5 months and 18 days.
  2. Total days: 4,187.
  3. Whole weeks: 598 (4,187 ÷ 7 = 598, remainder 1).
  4. Approximate months: 137.56 (4,187 ÷ 30.436875).
  5. Approximate years: 11.464 (4,187 ÷ 365.2425).

Result: 11 years, 5 months and 18 days of employment; 4,187 total days.

Method

How it is calculated

Calendar duration counts completed years, then completed months, then the remaining days, on the real calendar. Leap days inside the span count as ordinary days.

  • Total days is the plain day count between the two dates.
  • Approximate months = total days ÷ 30.436875, the average length of a month. Approximate years = total days ÷ 365.2425, the average length of a year.
  • The approximate figures are estimates from the day count. For contracts and records, use the calendar duration.
  • The span is continuous. Breaks, unpaid leave and part-time patterns inside it are not removed.
  • This tool measures one unbroken span between two dates. For completed service counted toward milestones, with the next anniversary, use the Years of Service Calculator in the related calculators below.
Reading your result

Reading your result

The calculator returns the calendar duration and the day-count figures behind it.

  • Calendar duration: Years, months and days between the two dates. This is the primary answer.
  • Total days: The plain day count. Use it to check the duration by hand.
  • Whole weeks: Total days ÷ 7, rounded down, with the remainder dropped.
  • Approximate months and years: Estimates from the day count. They are not calendar counts, so they can differ slightly from the calendar duration.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • One unbroken span only. For a career with gaps, measure each job separately and add the results.
  • Breaks, unpaid leave and part-time patterns inside the period are not removed.
  • Approximate months and years are estimates, not calendar counts.
  • It does not apply any pension, long-service award or contract rules. Check those with the scheme.
  • It treats the end date as the last day counted. A job that ends today needs today’s date, not yesterday’s.
Common questions

Employment Duration Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

What does the result include?

The span between the two dates is reported as years, months and days, together with the total number of days, the whole weeks, and approximate month and year equivalents. A start of 3 June 2019 and an end of 20 October 2026 gives 7 years, 4 months and 17 days, which is 2,696 total days.

How are the approximate year and month figures worked out?

They come from the total day count rather than the calendar. Total days are divided by 365.2425, the average length of a year, and by 30.436875, the average length of a month, so the 2,696-day example also reads as about 7.38 years and 88.58 months.

Can I measure a career made up of several jobs?

Not in one run: the calculator measures a single unbroken span between two dates and cannot combine separate jobs. For a career with gaps, measure each job separately with this tool and add the results together.

Does it subtract breaks, leave or part-time periods inside one job?

No. The span is counted as continuous calendar time from the start date to the end date, exactly as entered. Breaks, unpaid leave and part-time patterns inside the period are not removed, which is why the limits on the page say so.

Can I use this duration for a pension or long-service award?

Treat it as the calendar measure only. Whether a given period counts toward a specific award, pension or contract term depends on the rules of that scheme, and this calculation does not apply those rules, so check the fine print of the scheme first.
