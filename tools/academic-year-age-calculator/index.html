How it works

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the date of birth — the child's full birth date.
  2. Enter the school-year reference date — usually the first day of the school year or an admissions cutoff.
  3. Calculate — the browser counts whole years, then whole months, then the remaining days.
  4. Read, copy or print — the age in years, months and days, with total days, whole weeks, the Y;M;D format, the next birthday and the days until it.
Worked example

Worked example: born 3 March 2020, on 1 September 2026

A birth date of 3 March 2020 and a reference date of 1 September 2026, run through this page's own engine.

1. Full years3 March 2020 → 3 March 20266 years6 years, 0 months, 0 days

Total elapsed3 March 2020 → 1 September 20262,373 days339 whole weeks

The child is 6 years, 5 months and 29 days old when the school year starts on 1 September 2026. That is still 6 in completed years. The child turns 7 on 3 March 2027, which is 183 days after the reference date. Enter those two dates and the calculator returns the same figures.

Method

Method: completed calendar age at a date you choose

The engine is the site's chronological-age method, pointed at the reference date instead of today. It counts whole years to the last birthday anniversary, then whole months, then the remaining days. It uses real Gregorian month lengths, so a month is 28, 29, 30 or 31 days depending on where it falls — never a fixed 30 days. Dates are UTC calendar dates, so no time zone shifts the result. The total days are the plain calendar difference between the two dates, and whole weeks are that total divided by seven, discarding the remainder.

A child born on 29 February has the anniversary clamped to 28 February in non-leap years. A child born 29 February 2020 is counted as turning 6 on 28 February 2026. The next birthday is the next occurrence of the birth month and day after the reference date, with the same clamp. The days-until-birthday count runs from the reference date to it.

Reading your result

Reading your result

The result panel reports the age in several forms:

  • Age in years, months and days — the headline answer for the school-year date.
  • Total days — the exact calendar span from the birth date to the reference date.
  • Whole weeks — total days divided by seven, with the remainder dropped.
  • Y;M;D format — the same age as two-digit years, months and days, the format assessment forms use.
  • Next birthday — the date of the next birthday and the days remaining until it.
  • Born on — the weekday of the birth date, for checking the input.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • The calculator reports an age, not an eligibility decision. Which year group a child enters depends on the cutoff date and the rules of the school or authority.
  • It uses one reference date for the whole calculation. If a school uses different cutoffs for different year groups, run it once per cutoff.
  • A 29 February birthday is counted from 28 February in non-leap years here. A school that uses 1 March instead will differ by one day near the cutoff.
  • The result is only as accurate as the birth date entered. Check it against the birth record before putting the age in a form.
Common Questions

Academic Year Age Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

What age does this calculator report?

The exact age on a reference date you choose, typically the first day of a school year or an admissions cutoff, in years, months and days, together with the total days and the next birthday.

How old is a child born 15 October 2019 on 1 September 2026?

6 years, 10 months and 17 days: still 6 in completed years when the school year starts, turning 7 that October. The span is 2,513 total days.

Does the result decide which year group a child enters?

No. The calculator reports an age, not an eligibility decision: which year group a child enters depends on the cutoff date and rules of the school or authority, so set the reference date to that cutoff.

How is the age counted?

In completed calendar units from the birth date to the reference date: whole years to the last birthday anniversary, then whole months, then the remaining days. It is the chronological-age method used across this site, pointed at the school-year date you choose instead of today.

What happens for a child born on 29 February?

The anniversary is clamped to the last day of February in a non-leap year: a child born 29 February 2020 is counted as turning 6 on 28 February 2026.
