How it works

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter the joining date — use the official first day of active employment, as shown on the contract or onboarding record.
  2. Choose the reference date — keep Today for current tenure, or pick a date for a past role, a resignation, or a future projection.
  3. Decide whether to include the end date — tick the box only if the last day was a full paid day of service; it adds exactly one day.
  4. Calculate and read the result — the card shows completed years, months and days first, then total days, decimal years, working days and the next anniversary.

Method

Method: calendar-anniversary arithmetic

The engine counts in completed calendar units, never by dividing by 30 or 365. It first counts whole years to the last joining-date anniversary, then whole months, then the remaining days. When the day count goes negative, it borrows from the previous calendar month, so February is handled by its real length of 28 or 29 days. All dates are normalised to local midnight before subtraction.

Total elapsed days are the plain calendar difference between the two midnights. Decimal years divide that total by 365.2425, the average length of a Gregorian year, and are shown to two decimal places. Working days count Mondays to Fridays from the joining date up to, but not including, the reference date; public holidays are not subtracted. The next anniversary is the next occurrence of the joining month and day, clamped to 28 February in a non-leap year for a 29 February joining date. Breaks in service are never credited automatically: each term is calculated on its own and the totals are added through the additional service period fields.

Reading your result

Reading your result

The result card gives the same tenure in four forms. Use the form your reader asks for:

  • Completed years, months and days — the headline answer for resumes, service certificates and recognition letters.
  • Total elapsed days — the exact day count payroll and benefits teams use to check probation or leave accrual.
  • Decimal years — total days divided by 365.2425, the figure severance and pension formulas usually want.
  • Working days — an estimate of Mondays to Fridays only; it excludes weekends but not public holidays.
  • Next work anniversary — the date and the days remaining until the next joining-date anniversary.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • It measures calendar tenure only. Qualifying service for pensions, gratuity or severance follows employer policy and labour law, which can deduct leave or require minimum hours.
  • Working days are an estimate: weekends are excluded, but public holidays and part-time schedules are not.
  • A break in service is not detected. Enter each term separately and add the totals; the gap between terms is never counted.
  • The result is only as accurate as the dates entered. A joining date taken from memory instead of the contract can shift every figure.
  • Decimal years are an actuarial convenience, not a calendar duration. Do not quote 6.69 years where a contract asks for completed years and months.
Common Questions

Years of Service Calculator FAQ

Clear answers to common questions about calculating tenure, month lengths, rehire rules, and legal distinctions.

How is years of service calculated?

Years of service is calculated by taking your official joining date and measuring elapsed time to your reference date. First, count completed calendar years, then remaining whole calendar months, and finally the residual days.

Does the calculator include the end date?

By default, calendar interval subtraction is exclusive of the end date. If you check 'Include the end date (+1 day)', the calculation counts your final day as a completed working day.

How are decimal years calculated?

Decimal years divide total elapsed calendar days by 365.2425 (the average days in a Gregorian calendar year accounting for quadrennial leap years).

How does a leap year affect service calculation?

A leap year adds February 29 (366 days in the year), increasing the total day count by 1. However, full annual calendar anniversaries remain intact on your joining calendar date.

What if my start date was February 29?

In non-leap years, February 29 does not occur. Under standard calendar rules, your anniversary is marked either on February 28 or March 1 depending on organizational policy.

How do breaks in service affect my tenure?

A calendar calculator measures continuous elapsed duration between two dates. If you left and rejoined, each term is calculated separately and summed, excluding the break period.
