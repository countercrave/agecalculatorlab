Worked example

Worked example

From 10 January 2026 to 20 October 2026. Enter those two dates above and the calculator returns the same midpoint.

  1. Days between the two dates: 283.
  2. First half: 141 days (283 ÷ 2, rounded down).
  3. Second half: 142 days.
  4. Midpoint: 10 January 2026 + 141 days = 31 May 2026.
  5. 31 May 2026 is a Sunday.

Result: The midpoint of 10 January 2026 and 20 October 2026 is Sunday, 31 May 2026, with halves of 141 and 142 days.

Method

How it is calculated

Midpoint = the average of the two dates, taken as instants in UTC, date-only. The span is counted in whole days, so no time zone or daylight-saving shift can move the answer.

  • Span in days = end date − start date.
  • First half = span ÷ 2, rounded down. Second half = span ÷ 2, rounded up.
  • For an odd span, the true halfway moment falls at midday. The calculator reports that calendar date.
  • Order does not matter. If both dates are the same, the midpoint is that date.
Reading your result

Reading your result

The calculator returns four rows. Each one answers a different question about the split.

  • Midpoint date: The middle date. Use it to split a period into two halves.
  • Elapsed days: The full span between the two dates, in days.
  • First half and second half: The day count on each side of the midpoint. They differ by one when the span is odd.
  • Midpoint weekday: The weekday of the middle date, for planning around it.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • For odd spans, the calculator reports a date, not the exact midday moment.
  • Whole days only. Times of day are ignored.
  • It does not split by working days, weekends or holidays.
  • Both inputs are dates. It cannot split a period given only in years or months.
Common questions

Date Midpoint Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

How is the midpoint between two dates calculated?

The calculator averages the two dates: it counts the days between them, halves that number, and adds it to the start date. From 1 January to 20 July 2026 is 200 days, so the midpoint is 100 days in: 11 April 2026.

What happens when the span has an odd number of days?

The true halfway moment falls at midday. The calculator reports a calendar date: for 1 March to 8 March 2026 (7 days) it shows 4 March, with a 3-day first half and a 4-day second half.

Does it matter which date I enter first?

No. The two dates can be swapped without changing the result.

What if both dates are the same?

The midpoint is simply that date.

Do time zones or daylight saving affect the result?

No. Dates are treated as date-only values, so time of day and daylight-saving changes never shift the answer.
