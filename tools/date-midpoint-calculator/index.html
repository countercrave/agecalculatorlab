How the midpoint between two dates is found

Enter a start date and an end date, in either order. The calculator finds the calendar date exactly halfway between them and reports it together with the total elapsed days and the length of each half — useful for project timelines, anniversaries, and scheduling around a central date.

★Method

The average of the two dates

The calculator averages the two dates and converts the result back to a calendar date. Dates are treated as date-only values, so time of day and daylight-saving changes never shift the answer.

◎Example

Worked example: a 200-day span

From 1 January 2026 to 20 July 2026 is exactly 200 days. Half of 200 is 100, and 100 days after 1 January is 11 April 2026 — the midpoint the calculator returns, with 100 days in each half.

Odd spans and other boundaries

Method & limits

What this result means

A useful answer includes both the number and its assumptions.

Calculation method

Date Midpoint Calculator calculates the exact midpoint date between two calendar dates using Gregorian calendar arithmetic. Dates are treated as date-only values, avoiding accidental time-of-day and daylight-saving shifts.

Important: Weekend, holiday, legal, medical, financial, school or workplace policies are included only when the tool explicitly asks for them. Confirm official decisions with the responsible authority.

Odd spans: which half gets the extra day

The calculator counts the elapsed days between the two dates, halves that count, and adds the result to the start date. On an even span the halves are equal. On an odd span the count cannot be split evenly, so the whole days are divided as evenly as possible — the first half takes the smaller share — and the exact halfway moment falls at midday on the date shown. That is why the 1 to 8 March example produces halves of 3 and 4 days rather than 3.5 and 3.5. Note the counting convention: the start date is day zero and the count measures elapsed time. A plan or contract that counts both the first and the last day as full days is working with a span one day longer, and its middle day can fall one day later than the date shown here — check which convention your document uses before treating the midpoint as a deadline.

Time of day is ignored, on purpose

Both inputs are calendar dates, and each is treated as a whole day. Daylight-saving changes make some local days 23 or 25 hours long in clock time, but they never change how many calendar days sit between two dates, so they never move the midpoint. If an interval is genuinely defined by timestamps — a 72-hour equipment hire, a connection measured in hours — its halfway instant can sit up to half a day away from the date midpoint. For anything planned in whole days, which is what this tool is for, the date midpoint is exact.

Halfway between two dates is not the middle of a list

With two dates, the midpoint and the average are the same thing. With several dates they are not. The halfway point of a range — halfway between the earliest and the latest date — usually differs from the median, the date in the middle of the list. For events on 1, 2 and 31 January, the range midpoint is 16 January while the median is 2 January. This calculator returns the range midpoint of the two dates you enter; the middle value of a longer list cannot be found by entering its first and last dates here.

Edge cases worth knowing

  • Leap years: a span that includes 29 February is one day longer than the same calendar span in a common year. 1 February to 1 March is 29 days in 2028 and 28 days in 2026, and the full leap-year span from 1 January 2028 to 1 January 2029 (366 days) has its midpoint on 2 July 2028.
  • Crossing a year boundary: nothing changes in the arithmetic. 15 November 2026 to 15 February 2027 is 92 days, and the midpoint of that span is 31 December 2026.
  • Reversed order: entering the later date first returns the same midpoint — the pair is ordered before it is halved.
  • The same date twice: the span is zero days and the midpoint is simply that date.

Common questions

Date Midpoint Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, odd spans, order and boundaries.

How is the midpoint between two dates calculated?

The calculator averages the two dates: it counts the days between them, halves that number, and adds it to the start date. From 1 January to 20 July 2026 is 200 days, so the midpoint is 100 days in: 11 April 2026.

What happens when the span has an odd number of days?

The true halfway moment falls at midday. The calculator reports a calendar date: for 1 March to 8 March 2026 (7 days) it shows 4 March, with a 3-day first half and a 4-day second half.

Does it matter which date I enter first?

No. The two dates can be swapped without changing the result.

What if both dates are the same?

The midpoint is simply that date.

Do time zones or daylight saving affect the result?

No. Dates are treated as date-only values, so time of day and daylight-saving changes never shift the answer.
