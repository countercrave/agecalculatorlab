Every Age in the Group Counts

An average is never one person's age — it is the whole group in a single number. The youngest, the oldest and everyone in between each pull the mean toward themselves, which is why one very old or very young member can move it so much. Your own entries, sorted, are in the table alongside.

How it works

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose how to enter the group — type ages directly, or switch to Dates of Birth if you have a roster or class list.
  2. Add one entry per person — use the add button for larger groups and Remove to delete a row; blank rows are ignored, never counted as zero.
  3. Check the reference date in date-of-birth mode — every age is calculated as of that one date, so the average stays consistent.
  4. Decide whether to show the median — it is ticked by default and is most useful when the group spans a wide age range.
  5. Calculate — the result shows the mean to two decimal places, with the youngest, oldest, range and number of people counted.

Method

Method: the arithmetic mean, on exact ages

In ages mode the mean is the sum of every valid age divided by the number of valid entries, shown to two decimal places. The median is the middle value of the sorted ages, or the mean of the middle pair when the group has an even count. The range is the oldest age minus the youngest. Ages must be between 0 and 150, decimals are allowed, and blank rows are ignored — they are never counted as zero. An invalid entry produces a message naming the row to fix. Invalid means a negative age, an age over 150, or a birth date after the reference date. It is never silently included.

In dates-of-birth mode each person is first converted to an exact age on one shared reference date. That age is completed years plus the fraction of the current year of age. The fraction is the days since the last birthday divided by the days in that year of age. A 29 February birthday is observed on 28 February in non-leap years. Only then are the exact ages averaged. The calculator never averages completed whole-year ages alone, because rounding every age down first would drag the mean down. The total shown in the result is summed from the unrounded exact ages. Adding the two-decimal values in a table can therefore differ by 0.01 from the total. That is rounding, not an error.

Reading your result

Reading your result

The result panel reports the group in six figures. Each one answers a different question:

  • Mean age — the headline answer: the total of all ages divided by the number of people, to two decimal places.
  • People counted — how many valid entries went into the mean; blanks and invalid rows are not in this count.
  • Youngest and oldest — the smallest and largest ages in the group, as entered or as calculated.
  • Median age — the middle age when the group is sorted; shown when the median box is ticked.
  • Age range — oldest minus youngest, the spread of the group in a single number.
  • Sorted table — every entry in ascending order, the view the median is read from.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • The mean is a summary, not a description of any one person. A group can average 37 years without anyone in it being 37.
  • One very old or very young member pulls the mean toward them. When the group spans a wide range, read the median alongside the mean.
  • The result describes the group as entered. It does not determine eligibility for school, sport, employment or benefits — those use each person's exact age on the governing cutoff date.
  • Ages are capped at 150 and the group must contain at least one valid entry. Dates of birth after the reference date are rejected.
  • Two-decimal display can hide small differences: two groups with the same displayed mean can have slightly different exact means.
Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an average age calculator?

An average age calculator finds the arithmetic mean of a group of ages. It adds all the valid ages together and divides the total by the number of people included, then reports supporting statistics such as the youngest and oldest ages and the age range.

How do I calculate the average age of a group?

Add all the ages together and divide the total by the number of people included. For example, the mean of 20, 30, and 40 is (20 + 30 + 40) ÷ 3 = 30 years. This calculator does the same arithmetic for any group size, from two entries to a full class or team list.

Can I calculate average age from dates of birth?

Yes. Switch to the Dates of Birth mode, enter each date of birth, and set one shared reference date. The calculator works out each person's exact age on that date — completed years plus the fraction of the current year — and then averages those ages. Every age is measured on the same date, so the result is consistent.

What is the formula for average age?

Average age = the sum of all included ages divided by the number of valid entries: Mean = Σaᵢ ÷ n, where aᵢ is each person's age and n is the number of people. For ages 18, 24, 31, 42, and 55, that is 170 ÷ 5 = 34 years.

What is the difference between average age and median age?

Average age usually means the arithmetic mean: every value counts, so unusually high or low ages pull the result toward them. Median age is the middle value after sorting, so it resists outliers. For ages 20, 22, 23, 24, and 71, the mean is 32 years but the median is 23 years. Tick “Also show median age” to see both side by side.
