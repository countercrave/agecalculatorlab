What you get

How the brain-age estimate is built

Enter your chronological age plus four lifestyle answers: average sleep hours, active days per week, resting heart rate (optional) and smoking status. The calculator starts from your age and adds or subtracts a few years for each answer, then reports the result in years. It is an educational estimate for curiosity only — not a medical measurement of your brain.

♡Method

Small fixed adjustments

Each answer moves the estimate by a small fixed amount. Sleeping 7 to 9 hours subtracts a year, while under 6 or over 9 hours adds two. Five or more active days a week subtract two, and one or none adds two. A resting heart rate under 65 subtracts one and over 90 adds two. Smoking adds four years, or one for a former smoker. The adjustments are summed and the total is rounded.

◎Example

Worked example: age 40 with favourable answers

A 40-year-old who sleeps 8 hours, is active 5 days a week, has a resting heart rate of 60 and does not smoke scores −1, −2 and −1 for the first three answers and 0 for smoking — an adjustment of −4, giving an estimate of 36 years. Less favourable answers move it the other way: age 30 with 5 hours of sleep, no active days, a heart rate of 95 and smoking adds 10, for an estimate of 40.

⌁Limits

An estimate with hard edges

The scale is deliberately coarse: answers just either side of a cut-off, such as sleeping 6.9 versus 7 hours, can differ by years, and the estimate never drops below 13. It uses only the five answers entered — no memory tests, medical history or clinical measures — so treat it as a prompt to reflect on habits, not a diagnosis or a substitute for medical advice.
