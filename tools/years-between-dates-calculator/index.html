What you get

How whole years between two dates are counted

Enter a start date and an end date. The calculator counts the complete calendar years between them — a year counts only once its anniversary date has been reached — and reports the whole years together with the remaining months and days, plus the total days and whole weeks in the span.

▦Method

Complete anniversaries, not a day count

The headline figure is the number of whole years: the calculator steps forward from the start date anniversary by anniversary, then counts the leftover whole months and days. Dividing the total days by 365.25 would give a slightly different figure, so that approximation is shown separately.

◎Example

Worked example: 1 January 2015 to 20 October 2026

That span is 11 years, 9 months, 19 days — 11 whole years, because the twelfth anniversary, 1 January 2027, has not been reached yet. The same span is 4,310 days in total, or 615 whole weeks.

⌁Limits

One day short, and other boundaries

A single day changes the headline figure: 15 June 2000 to 14 June 2026 is 25 whole years (25 years, 11 months, 30 days), and the count only reaches 26 on 15 June 2026 itself. The result is pure calendar arithmetic; pension, service or eligibility schemes that define a year differently need their own rules.

Common questions

Years Between Dates Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

Why does being one day short change the whole-years answer?

A year only counts once its anniversary date has been reached. The span from 15 June 2000 to 14 June 2026 is 25 whole years (25 years, 11 months and 30 days), and the count reaches 26 only on 15 June 2026 itself.

Why not just divide the total days by 365.25?

Dividing by an average year length gives an approximation that can drift either side of the true anniversary count, especially near a boundary. This calculator counts complete anniversaries first and shows the average-based figure separately, so you can see both without confusing them.

What are the months and days shown after the whole years?

They are the leftover span after the last completed anniversary: whole calendar months counted from that anniversary, then the remaining days. In the page's worked example, 1 January 2015 to 20 October 2026 gives 11 whole years plus 9 months and 19 days.

What else does the result show besides whole years?

The same span is also reported as a total day count and in whole weeks. The worked-example span above is 4,310 days in total, which is 615 complete weeks.

Can I use this result for pension or service eligibility?

Treat it as calendar arithmetic only. Pension, service and eligibility schemes often define a qualifying year by their own rules, which may not match complete calendar anniversaries, so confirm the figure with the scheme before relying on it.
