How it works

How to use the Age Grading Calculator

Four short actions, in order. Nothing here repeats the lead or the method below.

1

Enter your age

Enter your age in years, from 5 to 99. The factor comes from the single-year table for that exact age, not an age band.

2

Choose sex and distance

Pick the sex category and one of ten road distances, from 1 mile to the 50K ultramarathon. Men and women have separate standards.

3

Enter your finish time

Enter the race finish time in hours, minutes and seconds. Use the same timing basis every time you compare runs.

4

Read the grade

The result shows the age-grade percentage, the level band, the age-graded standard time and your average pace per kilometre and per mile.

Worked example

Worked example: a 45-year-old man runs 42:30 for 10K

One runner, one race, every number traced to the 2025 WMA dataset (version 2025.1) loaded on this page.

  1. Open standard, men’s 10K: 1,584 seconds, which is 26 minutes 24 seconds.
  2. Age factor, men aged 45, 10K: 0.9261, from the 2025 WMA factor table.
  3. Age-graded standard: 1,584 ÷ 0.9261 = 1,710.4 seconds, which is 28 minutes 30 seconds.
  4. Race time: 42 minutes 30 seconds = 2,550 seconds.
  5. Age grade: (1,584 ÷ (2,550 × 0.9261)) × 100 = 67.07%.
  6. Average pace: 2,550 seconds over 10 km is 4 minutes 15 seconds per kilometre, and 6 minutes 50 seconds per mile.

Result: 67.07% · Local Class / Club.

Calculation method

How the Age Grading Calculator works

The grade compares your time with the open world standard, after adjusting for your age and sex with a single factor.

Formula: Age grade = open standard ÷ (race time × age factor) × 100

Dataset2025 USATF Masters LDR / WMA Road Running Age-Grade Tables, version 2025.1, compiled by Alan Lytton Jones and Tom Bernhard, approved January 2025.
Single-year factorsEach age from 5 to 99 has its own factor per distance and sex. A 45-year-old uses the 45 factor, not a 40–49 band.
Separate standardsMen and women have separate open standards and factors. The men’s 10K open standard is 1,584 seconds; the women’s is 1,726 seconds.
Road distances onlyTen certified road distances are covered: 1 mile, 5K, 5 miles, 8K, 10K, 10 miles, 15K, half marathon, marathon and 50K.
Reading your result

What each part of the result means

The result panel has ten rows. These are the ones that decide what the grade means.

  • Age grade score: the primary answer. In the example it is 67.07%.
  • Performance level: the band the score falls in. 67.07% is Local Class / Club, which covers 60.0% to 69.9%.
  • Your race time: the time you entered, shown as minutes and seconds. In the example it is 42:30.
  • Age-graded standard: 28:30 in the example. This is the open-division time your run equates to.
  • Open world standard: 26:24 for a men’s 10K. This is the unadjusted benchmark.
  • Age factor: 0.9261 in the example. This is the table factor for your exact age, sex and distance.
  • Average pace: 4:15 per kilometre and 6:50 per mile in the example.

Limits

What this tool does not do

Genuine limits only. If a limit matters for your decision, check it with the responsible authority.

  • Road running only. The tables do not cover trail, track or cross-country times.
  • The calculator grades the time entered. It cannot tell chip time from gun time, so use one basis consistently.
  • Course differences are not adjusted for. Hills, altitude and weather all affect the time but not the grade.
  • A grade above 100% is not an error. It means the run beat the open standard after the age adjustment.
  • Factors cover ages 5 to 99 only. Outside that range the calculator refuses the input.
Common questions

Age Grading Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, bands and responsible interpretation.

What is an age grade?

A percentage that compares your race time with the open world standard for your distance, adjusted for your age and sex. A 67.07% grade means your run is 67.07% of the age-adjusted open standard, using the 2025 WMA tables.

How is the 67.07% in the worked example calculated?

For a 45-year-old man running 42:30 for 10K: the men’s 10K open standard is 1,584 seconds and the age factor for 45 is 0.9261. The grade is (1,584 ÷ (2,550 × 0.9261)) × 100 = 67.07%, which falls in the Local Class / Club band.

Is 67.07% a good age grade?

It sits in the Local Class / Club band, which covers 60.0% to 69.9%. The bands run from below 60% (Recreational / Participant) through Regional Class at 70%, National Class at 80% and World Class at 90%, up to 100% and above at World Record Level.

Why do men and women get different grades for the same time?

Men and women have separate open standards and separate age factors in the WMA tables. For 10K the men’s open standard is 1,584 seconds and the women’s is 1,726 seconds, so the same finish time grades differently.

Does age grading work for a 5K or a marathon?

Yes. The 2025 tables cover ten road distances, from 1 mile to the 50K ultramarathon. Each distance has its own factors: for a 45-year-old man the factor is 0.9125 for 5K and 0.9261 for 10K.
