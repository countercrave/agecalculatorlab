Worked example

Worked example

14 February 2026, with a January fiscal-year start. Enter that date above and the calculator returns the same quarter end.

  1. February 2026 falls in Q1, which runs January to March.
  2. Quarter starts: 1 January 2026.
  3. Quarter ends: 31 March 2026.
  4. Days in the quarter: 90 (31 + 28 + 31).
  5. Days remaining from 14 February: 45.

Result: For 14 February 2026, Q1 ends on Tuesday, 31 March 2026.

Method

How it is calculated

Quarters are three-month blocks counted from the fiscal-year start month. The engine works in UTC, date-only, on calendar months.

  • Quarter number = months since the fiscal-year start ÷ 3, rounded down, plus 1.
  • Quarter end = the last calendar day of the third month in the quarter.
  • Quarter length runs from 89 to 92 days, depending on the months it covers. A quarter with February is the shortest.
  • Leap years add one day to any quarter that contains February.
  • Fiscal-year label: with an April start, a February date falls in fiscal year 2025/26, and its quarter is Q4.
  • 4-4-5 retail calendars and 13-period years are not modeled.
Reading your result

Reading your result

The calculator returns six rows. Each one answers a different question about the quarter.

  • Quarter: Which quarter the date falls in, Q1 to Q4.
  • Quarter starts: The first day of that quarter.
  • Quarter ends: The last day of that quarter. This is the primary answer.
  • Days in quarter: The full length of the quarter, in days.
  • Days remaining: Days from your date to the quarter end. It is 0 on the quarter end itself.
  • Fiscal year: The fiscal year the date belongs to, given your start month.

Limits

What this tool does not do

  • Calendar and fiscal quarters only. 4-4-5 retail calendars and 13-period years are not modeled.
  • The fiscal-year start must match your organization’s year. A wrong start month gives a wrong quarter.
  • Whole days only. Times of day are ignored.
  • It does not model holidays, business days or reporting deadlines.
Common questions

Quarter End Date Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

What does this calculator find?

Enter a date and the month your fiscal year starts in — January for calendar quarters. The calculator finds the quarter the date falls in and reports its final day, along with the quarter’s start date, its length in days and how many days remain in it.

How are the quarters counted?

Quarters are counted in three-month blocks from the fiscal-year start month. With an April start, for example, Q1 runs April to June and a date in October falls in Q3. The quarter end is always the last calendar day of the third month.

What quarter is 18 May 2026 in, and when does it end?

With a January start, 18 May 2026 falls in Q2, which ends on 30 June 2026 — 43 days after the entered date. With an April fiscal start, 20 October 2026 falls in fiscal Q3, ending 31 December 2026.

Are all quarters the same length?

No. Quarters are not all the same length: they run from 89 to 92 days depending on the months they cover — a quarter running February to April is 89 days in a common year — and a quarter containing February is a day longer in a leap year.

What if I enter a date that is itself a quarter end?

The days remaining is 0. The tool works in whole days and calendar months only; it does not model 4-4-5 retail calendars or 13-period years, and organisations define fiscal years differently, so confirm the start month against your own calendar.
