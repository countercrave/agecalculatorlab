How it works

Get your Pregnancy Due Date result in four clear steps

Enter the first day of your last period and your cycle length, then select Calculate. The explanation is broken into short actions so you can understand the workflow without reading a wall of text.

1

First day of last menstrual period

Enter the First day of last menstrual period (LMP). The due date is counted from this date, not from conception.

2

Calculate on

Enter the Average cycle length (days) — a 28-day cycle needs no adjustment — and leave Calculate on as today, or pick another reference date.

3

Select Calculate

The browser adds 280 days to the LMP, the standard 40-week estimate, then shifts the result by one day for every day your cycle differs from 28: due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length − 28). The result panel also shows the LMP you entered and the size of the cycle adjustment in days.

4

Review, copy or print

Read the due date as an estimate: an LMP of 1 January 2026 with a 28-day cycle gives 8 October 2026, and the same LMP with a 32-day cycle adds 4 days, giving 12 October 2026. Only a minority of babies arrive on the estimated date itself, so use it for planning and rely on your care provider's dating for medical decisions.

How the due date is estimated

Enter the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP) and your average cycle length. The calculator adds 280 days to the LMP — the standard 40-week estimate — and shifts the result by one day for every day your cycle differs from 28 days.

◌Method

LMP plus 280 days, adjusted for cycle

Due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length − 28). A 28-day cycle needs no adjustment. The result panel also shows the LMP you entered and the size of the cycle adjustment in days.

◎Example

LMP 1 January 2026, 28-day cycle

280 days after 1 January 2026 is 8 October 2026, with no cycle adjustment. The same LMP with a 32-day cycle adds 4 days, giving 12 October 2026.

⌁Limits

An estimate, not a scan date

The method assumes ovulation fell on the standard cycle day implied by your cycle length; real cycles vary, and only a minority of babies arrive on the estimated date itself. Use it for planning, and rely on your care provider’s dating for medical decisions.

Common questions

Pregnancy Due Date Calculator FAQ

Answers about method, inputs, privacy, boundaries and responsible interpretation.

How is the due date calculated?

The calculator adds 280 days to the first day of your last menstrual period (LMP), the standard 40-week estimate, and then shifts the result by one day for every day your average cycle length differs from 28 days: due date = LMP + 280 days + (cycle length - 28). A 28-day cycle needs no adjustment.

What due date does an LMP of 1 January 2026 give?

With a 28-day cycle, 280 days after 1 January 2026 is 8 October 2026, with no cycle adjustment. The result panel also shows the LMP you entered and the size of the cycle adjustment in days.

How does my cycle length change the due date?

One day is added for each day your cycle is longer than 28 days, and one day is taken away for each day it is shorter. The same LMP of 1 January 2026 with a 32-day cycle adds 4 days, giving a due date of 12 October 2026.

Why might this estimate differ from a scan-based date?

The method assumes ovulation fell on the standard cycle day implied by your cycle length, and real cycles vary. Use the estimate for planning, and rely on your care provider's dating for medical decisions.

Will the baby arrive on the estimated due date?

Only a minority of babies arrive on the estimated date itself. Treat the date as an estimate for planning rather than a fixed prediction of the delivery day.
